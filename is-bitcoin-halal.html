Is Bitcoin Halal?

The most-asked question in Islamic finance right now. The scholarly debate has largely settled — most contemporary scholars say Bitcoin is permissible as a commodity and store of value. Here's the full picture for Gulf Muslims.

Crypode · Halal Crypto Guide · Updated 2026
🎧Listen to this article
SPEED

The short answer

The majority of contemporary Islamic scholars — including those at the AAOIFI, the International Islamic Fiqh Academy, and many Gulf-based institutions — consider owning and trading Bitcoin to be permissible (halal), provided you are engaging in spot transactions (buying and holding actual Bitcoin), not leveraged speculation or derivatives.

A minority of scholars — particularly some earlier voices — declared it haram, primarily on grounds of excessive speculation and lack of a physical commodity backing. However, as Bitcoin has matured into a trillion-dollar asset with global acceptance as a store of value and medium of exchange, the scholarly consensus has increasingly shifted toward permissibility.

The Gulf position (2026): Oman, UAE, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have all taken regulatory steps to recognize cryptocurrency as an asset class. The UAE's CBUAE and ADGM, the Saudi SAMA, and the Capital Market Authority of Oman have published frameworks treating crypto as a legitimate investment instrument — implicitly supporting the permissibility argument.

What scholars say — the halal arguments

Scholars who permit Bitcoin generally make the following arguments:

  • It has genuine utility and value. Bitcoin is accepted globally as a medium of exchange and store of value. Scholars who define mal (property) broadly — as anything of recognised value in a community — classify Bitcoin as mal mutaqawwam (legitimate property).
  • No intrinsic riba. Owning Bitcoin does not generate interest or involve borrowing. The coin itself does not pay a return — unlike an interest-bearing bond or savings account.
  • It functions like a commodity. Early Islamic jurists distinguished between money (which must be gold, silver, or widely accepted currency) and commodities (which can be anything of value). Bitcoin fits the commodity classification well.
  • It supports financial inclusion. Bitcoin allows Gulf expat workers and the unbanked to send money cheaply across borders — a welfare (maslaha) argument that weighs in its favour.

What scholars say — the haram arguments

Scholars who prohibited Bitcoin typically argued:

  • Excessive gharar (uncertainty). Early fatwas pointed to extreme price volatility as evidence of prohibitive speculation, similar to gambling.
  • No tangible backing. Some scholars argued that for an asset to be legitimate, it must be backed by a physical good or state authority (like gold or government currency).
  • Potential for maysir (gambling). When Bitcoin is traded purely for price speculation with no intent to hold it as an asset, some scholars treat this as analogous to gambling.

Most of these arguments have weakened as Bitcoin has become a mainstream global asset with regulatory recognition and genuine utility. The analogy to gambling is weaker when applied to an investor who holds Bitcoin as a long-term store of value — the same behaviour applied to gold or property would not be called gambling.

Bitcoin vs Ethereum vs altcoins — does it matter?

The halal ruling applies most clearly to Bitcoin, because Bitcoin is the most widely accepted, most liquid, and most understood cryptocurrency. Ethereum and other major coins with genuine utility (smart contracts, DeFi infrastructure) are also generally considered permissible on the same basis.

The situation is different for meme coins, gambling-linked tokens, and tokens for projects with no real utility. Those are harder to justify under Islamic principles, as they resemble speculation on something with no intrinsic value — closer to pure maysir.

Practical guidance for Gulf Muslims

  • Buy and hold (spot only). Purchase Bitcoin directly on a reputable exchange — Binance and Bybit both offer spot trading with Islamic Account options for Gulf users. Do not use leverage, margin, or derivatives.
  • Avoid buying to purely day-trade. If your intent is to flip Bitcoin daily with no genuine investment thesis, you are closer to speculative gambling. Buy with an investment horizon.
  • Do your research on the coin. Stick to assets with genuine utility — Bitcoin, Ethereum — rather than tokens whose only purpose is to go up in price.
  • Pay zakat on your holdings. If your Bitcoin holdings exceed the nisab and have been held for a hawl year, zakat is due. See our full zakat on crypto guide.
  • Consult a scholar you trust. Islamic jurisprudence is not monolithic. If you have doubts, consult a qualified scholar in your country for a personalised ruling.
